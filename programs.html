PURPOSE & CONTEXT

Addressing Macro MSME Disparities & Local Rural Bottlenecks

National MSME census data indicates that out of 26.1 million MSMEs in India, only 13.85% are women-owned, with 98% concentrated in survivalist micro-enterprises due to credit and skill hurdles. In Sirivella Block, DWA field surveys identified five critical bottlenecks that trap Dalit women in debt bondage:

BOTTLENECK 01

Inefficient Record-Keeping

Financial transactions tracked informally or not at all, leaving women without verifiable business data.

BOTTLENECK 02

Lack of Formal Credit

Commercial banks refuse credit without documented transaction histories or physical collateral.

BOTTLENECK 03

Limited Formal Savings

Physical distance to bank branches and social barriers force cash hoardings vulnerable to loss.

BOTTLENECK 04

Digital Reluctance

Low digital literacy and fear of financial fraud restrict transactions solely to physical cash.

BOTTLENECK 05

Enterprise Skill Gap

Profits are absorbed into household consumption rather than reinvested in inventory or scaling.

Standardized 6-Stage Operational Framework

[1. Scoping] ➔ [2. Selection] ➔ [3. Curriculum] ➔ [4. Deployment] ➔ [5. Handholding] ➔ [6. M&E]
STAGE 01

Livelihood & Business Scoping

Exhaustive field surveys analyzing value chains, local demand, traditional artisan skills, and raw material access across Sirivella Block villages.

STAGE 02

Beneficiary Selection

Community assemblies in coordination with rural SHGs. Prioritizing Below Poverty Line (BPL), single, and widowed women aged 25–50 through structured screening.

STAGE 03

Curriculum Customization

Development of dialect-tailored workbooks and pictorial modules demystifying digital banking, passbook reconciliation, and micro-enterprise operations.

STAGE 04

Resource Deployment

Deploying 2 dedicated Master Trainers and a Project Officer for 6 months of hands-on daily technical instruction in leased village community training rooms.

STAGE 05

3-Phase Skill Building

Executing financial literacy (UPI/debit cards), business planning (pricing, packaging, Udyog Aadhaar), and livelihood trade asset handholding.

STAGE 06

Baseline & M&E Auditing

Pre-intervention baseline vs. 6- and 12-month post-intervention auditing of household revenues, bank savings, and micro-business sustainability.

3-Phase Structured Training Curriculum

01

Financial Education & Digital Banking

  • • Household income vs. business expense separation
  • • Passbook recording and savings bank account maintenance
  • • ATM / debit card operation and confidential PIN security
  • • Active QR code and UPI mobile payments for village retail
02

Business Planning & Management

  • • Principles of micro-enterprise and inventory rotation
  • • Wholesale procurement vs. retail margin calculation
  • • Customer retention, product packaging, and branding
  • • Formal statutory registration under Udyog Aadhaar MSME
03

Livelihood Skill Upgradation

  • • Specialized tailoring: designer blouses, school uniforms
  • • Scientific livestock care, feed management & vaccination
  • • Micro-grocery stock optimization and credit recovery
  • • Direct market linkages with town wholesalers and exhibitions

LEGAL & STATUTORY COMPLIANCE

Privacy Policy

LAST UPDATED: JANUARY 2026

The Dalit Welfare Association (DWA) is firmly committed to protecting the privacy, confidentiality, and security of donors, beneficiaries, volunteers, and website visitors.

1. Information Collection

We only collect personal information (name, email address, postal address, PAN number for 80G tax receipts, phone number) when voluntarily submitted by donors or newsletter subscribers.

2. Use of Information

Collected details are strictly used to issue official statutory tax-deductible receipts, comply with Government of India (Income Tax Dept & Ministry of Home Affairs FCRA) regulations, and deliver requested program updates. DWA never sells, trades, rents, or exchanges donor data with third parties.

3. Data Security

All donation transactions processed through accredited portals (Give.do, Benevity) employ industry-standard 256-bit SSL encryption. We maintain appropriate physical, electronic, and procedural safeguards.

4. Contact Officer

For any queries or data removal requests, please write to our Project Officer at dalitwelfareassociation1@gmail.com.

DONOR ASSURANCE · STATUTORY REFUND

Donation Refund & Cancellation Policy

LAST UPDATED: JANUARY 2026

The Dalit Welfare Association (DWA) is a non-profit voluntary society registered under the Societies Registration Act XXI of 1860. All contributions directly support charitable programs for rural poverty alleviation, women’s livelihood, and child education.

1. Voluntary Contributions

Donations once made are voluntary and non-refundable once statutory tax exemption certificates (80G receipts or FCRA returns) are generated.

2. Erroneous or Duplicate Donations

If an erroneous deduction, technical duplicate transaction, or accidental charge occurs, the donor must notify DWA within 7 working days of the transaction date. Please submit the transaction reference, payment screenshot, donor name, and banking proof to dalitwelfareassociation1@gmail.com.

3. Refund Processing

Upon verification, approved refunds will be credited back to the original source account/card via the banking channel within 10 to 14 business days, in compliance with applicable banking and RBI regulations.
